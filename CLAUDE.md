## Hard constraints

- **Local, CPU only.** No Colab, no server. Windows, macOS (Intel and Apple
  Silicon), Linux.
- **Processed public data only.** No FASTQ/BAM processing. Downloads happen in
  the notebooks from the original sources.
- **No model training.** Settled 2026-09-23: no GPU, too much complexity.
  Pretrained CREsted models only. This closes the former project 3 option (a).
- **Beginner-friendly notebooks**, with executed copies saved alongside.
- **Memory:** assume 8 GB RAM.
- **Environment:** `uv` + Python 3.12 + CREsted + PyTorch (see `Setup.md`).

## Conventions for the notebooks

Settled; do not re-litigate:

- **Neutral scientific prose. No em-dashes.** Not promotional. The builders
  assert `"—" not in source` before writing.
- **The audience are scientists.** Say nucleotides, not letters. Explain
  programming concepts, not basic biology.
- **Standard library calls stay visible in the cells.** Participants must see
  `sc.read_h5ad`, `sc.pp.normalize_total`, `sc.pl.umap`,
  `sc.tl.rank_genes_groups`, `pyBigWig.open(...).stats(...)`,
  `crested.tl.predict` and so on, because that is what transfers to their own
  work. Helpers in `scripts/` are only for things with no standard equivalent
  (the track plot), preparation too heavy for a live cell (`prepare_data.py`),
  or lookup data (`celltypes.py`, `genes.py`).
- **RNA before ATAC.** Expression is the more familiar measurement.
- **Prefer plots to tables and printed output.**
- **Prefer regions the papers actually tested** over arbitrary accessible ones,
  so there is a known answer to check the model against.
- Notebooks are generated by builder scripts, then executed with
  `jupyter nbconvert --execute` into `notebooks/practicals/executed/`.

## Data (all verified)

### Gene expression

- **Fly Cell Atlas, 10x head, stringent** (Li, Janssens et al. 2022).
  `https://cloud.flycellatlas.org/index.php/s/LAEybPc2HZnpzKs/download`,
  **2.5 GB**. FCA has no brain tissue file; the head is closest and also
  contains eye, cuticle, muscle and fat.
  - `sc.read_h5ad` takes ~50 s and 2.5 GB, and `adata[mask].copy()` afterwards
    was **OOM-killed on a 17 GB machine**, because `.copy()` duplicates the
    attached `.raw` (13,056 x 100,527). No chance on 8 GB.
  - `scripts/prepare_data.py` reduces it once with h5py to
    `data/processed/fca_brain.h5ad`, **44 MB**, 30,789 brain cells, all 13,056
    genes. Loads in 0.4 s at 0.93 GB peak.
  - Subset brain with `annotation_broad in {neuron, glial cell}`.
    `annotation_broad_extrapolated` covers 100% of cells but admits cone cells
    and hemocytes.
  - **Already `normalize_total(1e4)` + `log1p`.** `expm1` sums to exactly 10,000
    per cell. Do not normalise again.
  - `var["flybase_mg__transcription_factors"]` flags 595 TFs.
- **Ravenscroft et al. 2019 larval brain**, portal
  `downloads/loom/Ravenscroft_et_al_2019_LarvalBrain.loom`, 21 MB. Has
  neuroblasts and GMCs, absent from the adult. **Raw integer counts**, so this
  one does need normalising. Converted to `data/processed/larval_brain.h5ad`.
- **`sc.read_loom` cannot open the portal looms.** loompy installs but rejects
  them: the SCope-style looms store `Embedding` and `Clusterings` as compound
  dtypes, which Loom spec 2.0 disallows. Read with h5py, write h5ad. The old
  note in this file saying to use `sc.read_loom()` was wrong.
- Davie et al. 2018 adult brain loom (519 MB) is no longer used; FCA replaced
  it, and FCA's cell type names are self-documenting where Davie's are codes.

### Chromatin accessibility

1. **Janssens et al. 2022 adult brain** (GEO GSE163697), portal
   `https://flybrain.aertslab.org` (https works; http redirects).
   - bigwigs `downloads/bigwig/AdultCellTypes/<name>.bw`, 82 cell types,
     **~58 MB each**
   - peaks `downloads/peaks/AdultCellTypes/<name>.bed`, 78 files, ~0.4 MB each
   - **The two directories disagree on names.** `KC_ab.bw` is `KC_a_b.bed`;
     `KC_abPrime.bw` is `KC_a__b_.bed`; `T4_a_b`, `T4_c_d`, `T5_a_b`, `T5_c_d`
     have a bigwig but no peak file. Handled by `PEAK_NAME` / `NO_PEAK_FILE`.
   - **`downloads/other/enhancersTested.bed`**, the 54 public enhancers they
     tested in vivo with reporter lines. Worth far more than arbitrary regions.
     Figure 4 of the paper dissects two of them:
     - `enh47` chr2L:20040501-20041072, near *sNPF*, gamma Kenyon cells,
       driven by **Ey** and **Mef2** sites (shown by mutagenesis)
     - `enh44` chr3R:21945795-21946260, near *Eip93F*, T4, **Fkh** and **TfAP-2**
     Widths 299-1,731 bp, so centre them for a 500 bp model. Practical 6 uses
     enh47 for its contribution score section.
   - whole-brain peaks `other/BrainPeaks_ResizedToMax500.bed` 4.8 MB;
     DARs `other/tbl_DARs.txt.zip` 18 MB
   - early development bigwigs `downloads/bigwig/EarlyDevelCellTypes/`, 53 cell
     types, larva to 12h APF. **Best choice for project 2**, because they share
     processing with the adult tracks and so compare directly.
2. **Merrill et al. 2022 TH vs GABA bulk ATAC** (GEO GSE197760), all four files
   confirmed at `https://ftp.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/series/GSE197nnn/GSE197760/suppl/`:
   TH-merged.bw 15.4 MB, GABA-merged.bw 16.5 MB, differential.csv.gz 5.0 MB,
   RAW.tar 7.5 MB.
3. **Dickmanken et al. 2026 embryo scATAC** (GEO GSE293575, Nat Commun
   10.1038/s41467-026-68742-4). Embryo 16-20 h AEL, **one stage only**.
   - bigwigs at `https://ucsctracks.aertslab.org/papers/hydrop_v2_paper/dm6/bw/drosophila_embryo_10x_v2_only/`,
     all 20 verified, **49 to 343 MB each**. No peak BED files.
   - three more filename mismatches against the model's class names, two of them
     typos on the server: `Malpighian_tubule`, `Pharnyx`,
     `muscle_attachement_Stripe`. Handled by `EMBRYO_FILE_NAME`.
   - fragments and count matrix at `https://resources.aertslab.org/papers/hydrop_v2/fly/`

### Genome

dm6 from UCSC `goldenPath/dm6/bigZips/dm6.fa.gz`, 140 MB unzipped.
`crested.Genome(path)` works directly; no chrom sizes file needed.
`resources/dm6_genes.tsv` holds 17,184 symbols with dm6 coordinates from UCSC
`ncbiRefSeqCurated`. Fly symbols are mixed case by FlyBase convention (`alrm`
but `Imp`), so lookups are case-sensitive and `scripts/genes.py` suggests the
right spelling on a miss.

## Models (both verified)

- **DeepFlyBrain**: `crested.get_model("DeepFlyBrain")` returns
  `(path, output_names)`. 12 MB, dm6, 500 bp in, **81 topic outputs**, 0.5 s per
  sequence. Outputs are `Topic_1..Topic_81`; **26 carry a cell type
  annotation**, scraped from the portal legend into
  `scripts/deepflybrain_topics.py`. Covers Kenyon cells, T neurons, glia.
- **Embryo10x**: `crested.get_model("Embryo10x")`. 34 MB, dm6, 500 bp in, **20
  outputs that are named cell types**, no lookup needed. Peak regression, so its
  outputs are predicted peak height and are **not** comparable in magnitude with
  DeepFlyBrain's probabilities. Only rankings within one model are interpretable.
- Neither has a dopaminergic class.

### Interpretation

- `crested.tl.contribution_scores(..., method="integrated_grad")` is the default
  choice: a fraction of a second per sequence.
- `method="mutagenesis"` (ISM) is more intuitive to explain but **too slow
  here**: 7.7 s for DeepFlyBrain and **39.5 s for Embryo10x** on one 500 bp
  sequence, and its output wants `method="mutagenesis_letters"` in the plot
  rather than a normal sequence logo. Not used in the practicals.
- `crested.pl.explain.contribution_scores(..., highlight_positions=[(s, e)],
  highlight_kws={...})` marks motif positions on the logo. Practical 6 uses it
  to box the Mef2 consensus matches.
- `scripts/motifs.py` scans a sequence for a named fly TF's motif, using the
  CREsted motif database (`crested.get_motif_db()`, 7,446 PWMs plus a
  `Fly_Direct_annot` mapping). Used to annotate contribution plots.
  - Motif ids for the Figure 4 factors are recorded in `FIGURE4_MOTIFS`. **Mef2
    has no fly motif in the database**, so the human MEF2A matrix is used; its
    id spells the consensus, `KCTAWAAATAGM`.
  - On enh47 this recovers **Mef2** (2.5x average importance) and **Onecut**
    (2.9x), both annotated in Figure 4, sitting on the two tallest contribution
    peaks. It does **not** recover Sr or Ey even at a 0.65 threshold, which is a
    real limitation of scanning for known motifs and is stated as such in the
    notebook.
- Discovering motifs with tf-modisco / TOMTOM is deliberately out of scope.
- Predicting all 54 tested enhancers takes seconds and gives a good menu of
  worked examples. Strongest per cell class: `enh52` gamma Kenyon cells 0.955
  (near *ab*), `enh19` T1 0.886 (near *Or67a*), `enh30` ensheathing glia 0.804
  (near *dally*). Note `enh44`, the Eip93F/T4 enhancer from Figure 4b, does
  **not** give a clean T4 call from DeepFlyBrain (best T4 topic 0.23, an
  unannotated topic dominates), so it is not used as a worked example.

### CREsted gotchas, all hit while building Practical 6

1. **`crested.tl`'s lazy import sets the matplotlib backend to `pdf`.** Every
   `plt.show()` afterwards renders nothing in Jupyter and only warns
   "FigureCanvasPdf is non-interactive". Silent failure. Fix: touch
   `crested.tl` and `crested.pl`, then `%matplotlib inline`, then import pyplot.
2. **Contribution scores crash on Apple Silicon.** They feed float64 to the
   model and MPS rejects it. Fix: `KERAS_TORCH_DEVICE=cpu` before importing
   Keras, an official Keras variable. `torch.set_default_device("cpu")` does
   **not** work.
3. `crested.pl.patterns.*` is deprecated; use `crested.pl.explain.*`.
4. `crested.tl.predict` rejects a numpy array of strings; pass a list.

Worth raising upstream, since Niklas maintains CREsted.

## Group projects

Each follows: look at accessibility -> find candidate enhancers near known genes
-> ask a model which motifs drive them -> check the TF's expression in
scRNA-seq -> present one biological story.

1. **What makes a neuron a neuron?** Janssens adult bigwigs and peaks; Kenyon
   cells vs T neurons vs glia; DeepFlyBrain contribution scores; TF expression
   in FCA. Best fit for DeepFlyBrain.
2. **How does the regulatory landscape change as the brain matures?** Janssens
   early development vs adult bigwigs, plus the larval brain h5ad. DeepFlyBrain
   only knows adult topics, so frame it as "are adult enhancers already open in
   the larva, and does the model recognise them?" Embryo10x is a second opinion.
3. **Dopaminergic neurons and Parkinson's.** Merrill TH vs GABA bigwigs and the
   differential table; look near *ple*, *DAT*, *Vmat*, *Lrrk*, *Pink1*, *park*,
   *Synj*. **Neither model has a dopaminergic class** and training one is ruled
   out, so this is motif enrichment only, or the "what a model cannot tell you"
   contrast. Practical 6 section 5 already sets that up.
